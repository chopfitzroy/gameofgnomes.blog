== Gameplay loop

I've been thinking a lot about the _gameplay loop_ lately, in this case I've been thinking about Shadowdark/5e specifically but in retrospect I think most of this is generally applicable.

One of the things I've noticed in my own game is I can really _feel it_ when a player doesn't feel they have any meaningful choices to make on their turn. Any time a player says something like _"I'm not really sure what to do so I'll just stay put"_ I know I've fucked up.

To be clear the issue here isn't deciding to do nothing the issue is deciding to do nothing when there is no alternative, for example it's very different if the player says _"I want to see how the argument between the merchant and the lord plays out so I will stay where I am and just listen in for now"_.

So how to I combat this in practice? I've come up with a four step process that I use to remind myself to present interesting choices to my players every round.

Disclaimer I typically use side initiative (see https://slyflourish.com/side_initiative.html[simpler initiative options]) at my table and only have players roll initiave checks to avoid being surprised for the first round in the rare situations where enemies get the drop on them, this isn't a requirement of the below steps but it certainly helps as your apply the process to the entire group as opposed to once for each individual.
